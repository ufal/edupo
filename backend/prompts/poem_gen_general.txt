## General Czech Poetic Approach

* **DRAW** from the broad tradition of Czech poetry rather than from the identifiable style of any single poet.

* **USE** the expressive possibilities of the Czech language naturally and creatively.

* **BALANCE** traditional poetic techniques with originality and contemporary linguistic naturalness.

* **DO NOT** attempt to imitate, reproduce, or closely approximate the recognizable style of any particular living or historical author.

* **DO NOT** invent an author-specific style profile unless the user explicitly provides one as part of the task.

* **DEVELOP** a distinctive poetic voice appropriate to the individual poem.

(Context: "The poem should feel like an original Czech poem, not like an imitation of a known literary figure.")

---

## Czech Language and Diction

* **WRITE** in natural, idiomatic, grammatically correct Czech.

* **USE** a rich and varied Czech vocabulary.

* **PREFER** precise and evocative words over generic or predictable poetic language.

* **USE** Czech linguistic features creatively where appropriate, including:

  * vowel and consonant sound
  * rhythm
  * word order
  * inflection
  * diminutives
  * repetition
  * alliteration
  * assonance
  * consonance
  * internal echoes
  * ambiguity
  * semantic contrast

* **PRESERVE** natural Czech syntax unless deliberate poetic inversion clearly improves the poem.

* **AVOID** vocabulary that sounds artificially archaic, translated, or unnatural unless the user's requested aesthetic calls for it.

---

## Imagery and Poetic Expression

* **USE** vivid imagery and concrete sensory details where appropriate.

* **EMPLOY** metaphor, simile, symbolism, personification, synesthesia, contrast, paradox, and other poetic devices when they genuinely contribute to the poem.

* **CREATE** meaningful relationships between images rather than simply accumulating decorative metaphors.

* **ALLOW** implication, ambiguity, and suggestion where they strengthen the emotional effect.

* **AVOID** excessive clichés, generic romantic imagery, empty abstractions, and decorative language without semantic purpose.

* **PREFER** original and specific images over familiar poetic formulas.

(Context: "Strong poetry should create an experience and atmosphere rather than merely describe an emotion.")

---

## Rhythm and Metre

* **PREFER** a recognizable poetic rhythm such as **trochee, iamb, or dactyl**, unless the user's parameters specify otherwise.

* **SELECT** the rhythmic approach that best suits the poem's subject, mood, and language.

* **MAINTAIN** rhythmic coherence throughout the poem where a regular metre is chosen.

* **ALLOW** controlled rhythmic variation when it improves naturalness, emphasis, tension, or emotional expression.

* **DO NOT SACRIFICE** meaning or natural Czech syntax merely to satisfy mechanical metrical regularity.

(Context: "Rhythm should serve the poem rather than make the poem sound mechanically constructed.")

---

## Rhyme and Sound

* **USE** rhyme only when it contributes meaningfully to the poem.

* When rhyme is used, **DO NOT RHYME VERSES WITH IDENTICAL WORDS**.

* **PREFER** different words that create convincing phonetic relationships.

* **AVOID** forced, predictable, or semantically weak rhymes.

* **CONSIDER** alternatives to end rhyme, including:

  * internal rhyme
  * assonance
  * consonance
  * alliteration
  * recurring sounds
  * rhythmic repetition

* **ADAPT** the use of rhyme and sound to the overall character of the poem.

---

## Emotional and Thematic Coherence

* **DEVELOP** a coherent emotional atmosphere.

* **ENSURE** that imagery, vocabulary, rhythm, and structure reinforce the poem's central theme.

* **AVOID** abrupt shifts in emotional register unless they are deliberate and artistically justified.

* **PREFER** subtle emotional expression over explicitly explaining every feeling.

* **SHOW** emotion through images, situations, sounds, contrasts, and carefully selected language whenever possible.

(Context: "The reader should be allowed to discover the emotion within the poem rather than having every emotion explicitly explained.")

---

## Originality

* **CREATE** an entirely original poem.

* **DO NOT** reproduce recognizable passages, stock verses, famous poetic lines, or memorized literary formulations.

* **DO NOT** construct the poem from quotations or near-paraphrases of existing poetry.

* **AVOID** clichés unless they are deliberately transformed into something fresh.

* **CREATE** original combinations of imagery, language, rhythm, and thought.
